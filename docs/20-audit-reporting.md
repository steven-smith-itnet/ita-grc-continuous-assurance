# External audit support and compliance reporting

**Objective:** provide a coherent, reproducible account of control objectives, evidence, findings and management action. The GRC team coordinates readiness and evidence under the GRC manager. The independent assessor retains responsibility for the external assessment conclusion.

## Prepare the engagement boundary

Agree service scope, legal entities, locations, relevant framework version, period, expected deliverables, evidence format, access arrangements and points of contact. Identify subservice organizations, inherited controls and customer responsibilities. Record changes to scope during the engagement.

Create a provided-by-client (PBC) register. Each request gets an ID, requirement/control reference, period, population, owner, due date, evidence location, classification, reviewer status and question history. Use the register as the coordination source across regions.

## Produce a workpaper

1. State the control objective and implementation.
2. Define the population and period, including additions/deletions.
3. Explain the test method and any sampling rationale.
4. Identify source systems and assess reliability/completeness of the supplied information.
5. Record evidence references, test execution and observed results.
6. Evaluate exceptions and distinguish design, implementation and operating issues.
7. State the conclusion within the actual test's limits.
8. Record preparer and independent reviewer names/dates.

Do not copy a provider's compliance badge into a workpaper as proof that the customer's service is compliant. Provider reports may support inherited-control analysis when their scope, period and complementary responsibilities fit the service.

## Walkthrough and fieldwork

Have the process owner demonstrate a real or approved synthetic transaction from start to finish: access requested, approved, provisioned, used, reviewed and removed. Trace evidence across systems. Ask what happens when automation fails or an emergency requires a bypass. Compare the documented procedure with actual operation.

Maintain a question log and answer consistently through the agreed contact. If a requested artifact is unavailable, identify the gap and propose a legitimate alternative for the assessor to evaluate. Do not manufacture backdated evidence or reconstruct approvals as if they existed at the time.

## Two report layers

**Management brief:** scope and period, major risk themes, material findings, coverage gaps, overdue decisions, remediation forecast and requested management actions. Use concise impact statements with named owners and dates.

**Technical appendix:** complete population, control version, test methodology, provider-specific observations, evidence index, state counts, exception details and retest records. Engineers need reproducible steps, not only a red status icon.

## Example synthetic narrative

“The lab assessed nine declared storage workloads against 14 control definitions. There were 108 applicable asset/control pairs. Of these, 87 passed, nine failed and 12 lacked sufficient fresh evidence. One failure has a synthetic approved exception and remains reported as a failure. The most immediate tasks are to address the known storage baseline failures and restore complete evidence collection. These are demonstration results and do not establish any organization's compliance.”

Those values are generated by the checked-in fixture. If the fixture changes, regenerate the report and update any presentation figures from the same output rather than typing independent numbers.

## Findings communication

Discuss draft findings with owners to validate facts and causes. Do not let management disagreement silently delete a supported condition. Record disagreements, revised evidence and the rationale for changes. Obtain management responses with actions, owners, dates and acceptance of residual risk where authorized.

## Good, better, best

Good: consistent PBC register, reviewed workpapers and clear reports. Better: reusable evidence contracts, period-based exports and automated completeness checks. Best: sustained evidence quality monitoring, trusted provenance and reduced manual collection with independent review. Faster evidence production is valuable only when the evidence remains relevant and reliable.
