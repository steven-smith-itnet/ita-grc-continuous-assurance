# Organizational, supplier, personnel and physical controls

**Objective:** cover the parts of compliance that cloud scanners cannot establish. A mature program connects technical results to leadership, people, facilities, suppliers and legal obligations.

## Governance and policy

Maintain an approved policy hierarchy with owner, scope, version, effective date, review cadence and exception process. Link standards to implementable control requirements and procedures to actual operators. Communicate changes and preserve acknowledgement or training evidence where required.

Risk assessments should identify business objectives, threat scenarios, existing controls, likelihood/impact rationale, treatment and residual-risk decisions. Management reviews trends, resource needs and unresolved issues. A risk register with no decisions or follow-through does not show effective oversight.

## Personnel lifecycle and training

Coordinate employment/contractor screening where appropriate, confidentiality obligations, role-specific training, acceptable-use expectations, disciplinary procedures and termination processes. Legal and HR own jurisdiction-sensitive decisions. TechGRC tests whether defined procedures operate and whether technical access lifecycle aligns with personnel events.

Training evidence includes target population, assignments, completion, exceptions and effectiveness checks. Completion percentage alone does not establish that administrators can execute a recovery runbook or recognize a targeted social-engineering attempt.

## Supplier and subservice organization assurance

1. Inventory suppliers and the services/data/privileges they access.
2. Tier them by criticality, data sensitivity, substitutability and integration risk.
3. Review contracts, security commitments, incident notification, data location, subprocessors, deletion and exit support.
4. Examine relevant independent reports for scope, period, exceptions and complementary customer controls.
5. Record inherited controls and the customer's remaining responsibilities.
6. Review material changes and monitor recurring obligations.
7. Test exit plans for critical providers, including data export and access revocation.

A supplier's report does not automatically cover the exact product, region or period the service uses. Record bridge information and unresolved gaps where relevant. Do not publish restricted supplier reports in a portfolio repository.

## Physical and environmental controls

Cloud providers operate many underlying facilities, but the customer may still operate offices, endpoints, network equipment, removable media and personnel processes. Identify inherited physical controls from appropriate provider assurance material and document retained customer responsibilities.

For customer-controlled locations, consider access authorization, visitor handling, equipment protection, environmental resilience, media custody and disposal. A cloud-only application can still be compromised through an administrator's unmanaged endpoint. Endpoint posture and workforce practices belong in the service risk picture.

## Asset disposal and records

Maintain equipment/media inventories, sanitization procedures, disposal evidence and chain of custody where appropriate. For cloud services, address resource deletion, residual snapshots, object versions, backups, keys and provider retention behavior. Records-management requirements may differ from operational backup retention.

## Privacy and contractual obligations

Identify data subjects, purposes, lawful processing decisions where applicable, notices, customer instructions, rights-request procedures, subprocessors and cross-border transfers. Legal/privacy specialists determine obligations, while engineering implements and tests approved requirements. Avoid treating a generic privacy framework mapping as a substitute for the actual contract or law.

## Manual evidence can still be engineered

Use structured forms with required fields, stable IDs, authenticated submitters, approvals and timestamps. Sample records against authoritative populations. Track overdue attestations and independently verify high-risk statements. A manual control is not inherently weak, and an automated control is not inherently reliable.

## Good, better, best

Good: owned policies and registers with reviewed evidence. Better: integrated HR, procurement, ITSM and training workflows with completeness checks. Best: sustained cross-functional assurance, measured effectiveness and tested supplier/people dependencies during exercises.

This chapter closes the organizational boundary around [the technical tracks](05-architecture.md). It does not claim that the 14 executable predicates cover every control required by the listed frameworks.
